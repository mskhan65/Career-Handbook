# 📚 Introduction

This dictionary contains the most important terms introduced throughout the **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** module.

It serves as a quick reference for:

- Learning new terminology
- Interview preparation
- Exam revision
- Understanding research papers
- Building a strong NLP vocabulary

The terms are arranged in **alphabetical order**.

---

# A

## AI (Artificial Intelligence)

A branch of computer science focused on building systems that can perform tasks requiring human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, understanding language, and decision-making.

---

## Alignment

Techniques used to make AI systems behave in ways that are more helpful, safe, and consistent with human preferences.

---

## Attention Mechanism

A technique that allows a model to focus on the most relevant parts of the input instead of treating every word equally.

---

## Autoregressive Model

A model that generates output one token at a time, where each new token depends on all previously generated tokens.

Example:

```text
Hello

↓

Hello World

↓

Hello World Again
```

---

# B

## Bag of Words (BoW)

A text representation technique that converts text into numerical vectors by counting word frequencies while ignoring grammar and word order.

---

## BERT

**Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers**

An encoder-only Transformer model designed primarily for understanding text.

---

## Bias

Systematic patterns learned from training data that may lead to unfair or inaccurate model behavior.

---

## Bidirectional Context

Understanding language by considering both the words before and after a given word.

---

# C

## Chatbot

A software application that interacts with users using natural language through text or speech.

---

## Context

The surrounding words or information that help determine the meaning of a word or sentence.

Example:

```text
Bank
```

may refer to:

- Financial institution
- River bank

depending on context.

---

## Corpus

A large collection of text documents used to train or evaluate NLP models.

---

# D

## Decoder

The Transformer component responsible for generating output text.

GPT is based on a decoder-only architecture.

---

## Deep Learning

A subset of Machine Learning that uses neural networks with multiple layers to learn complex patterns from data.

---

## Dense Vector

A numerical representation where most values are non-zero.

Word embeddings are dense vectors.

---

# E

## Embedding

A dense numerical representation of text that captures semantic meaning.

---

## Encoder

The Transformer component responsible for understanding and processing input text.

BERT is based on an encoder-only architecture.

---

## Entity

A meaningful object identified in text, such as a person, organization, location, or date.

Example:

```text
Google

Paris

2026
```

---

# F

## FastText

A word embedding algorithm developed by Meta that represents words using character-level subword information.

---

## Fine-Tuning

Adapting a pre-trained model to perform better on a specific task using additional training data.

---

# G

## Generative AI

Artificial Intelligence systems capable of creating new content such as text, images, audio, code, or video.

---

## GPT

**Generative Pre-trained Transformer**

A family of decoder-only Transformer models that generate text through next-token prediction.

---

## GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)

A recurrent neural network architecture that improves learning of long-term dependencies while being simpler than an LSTM.

---

# H

## Hallucination

A response generated by an AI model that is incorrect, fabricated, or unsupported by evidence.

---

## Hidden State

The internal memory used by an RNN to carry information from previous inputs through a sequence.

---

# I

## IDF (Inverse Document Frequency)

A measure of how rare or informative a word is across a collection of documents.

---

## Inference

The process of using a trained model to make predictions or generate outputs for new inputs.

---

# K

## Key (K)

In the Attention Mechanism, the representation used to determine how relevant one token is to another.

---

# L

## Language Model

An AI model that predicts or generates text based on learned language patterns.

---

## Lemmatization

The process of converting words to their dictionary (base) form.

Example:

```text
Running

↓

Run
```

---

## LLM (Large Language Model)

A language model trained on massive text datasets that can perform a wide variety of language-related tasks.

---

## LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)

A type of recurrent neural network that uses memory cells and gates to capture long-term dependencies more effectively than a standard RNN.

---

# M

## Masked Language Modeling (MLM)

A training objective used by BERT where some words are hidden, and the model predicts the missing words using surrounding context.

---

## Machine Learning

A branch of AI where computers learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed for every task.

---

## Multi-Head Attention

A mechanism that applies multiple attention operations in parallel, enabling the model to capture different types of relationships simultaneously.

---

# N

## Named Entity Recognition (NER)

An NLP task that identifies entities such as people, organizations, locations, dates, and more.

---

## Natural Language Generation (NLG)

The process of generating human-readable language from structured information or learned patterns.

---

## Natural Language Processing (NLP)

A branch of Artificial Intelligence that enables computers to understand, analyze, interpret, and generate human language.

---

## Natural Language Understanding (NLU)

The process of enabling computers to understand the meaning and intent of human language.

---

## Neural Network

A Machine Learning model inspired by the human brain, consisting of interconnected artificial neurons.

---

## Next-Token Prediction

The task of predicting the next token in a sequence.

GPT is trained using next-token prediction.

---

# P

## Parameter

A learned numerical value inside a Machine Learning model that is adjusted during training.

Large Language Models often contain billions of parameters.

---

## Positional Encoding

Additional information added to token embeddings so that Transformer models understand the order of tokens in a sequence.

---

## Pre-training

Training a model on a very large, general-purpose dataset before adapting it to specific tasks.

---

## Prompt

The text or instruction provided by a user to an AI model.

Example:

```text
Explain Machine Learning.
```

---

# Q

## Query (Q)

In the Attention Mechanism, the representation that determines what information the model is seeking.

---

# R

## Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)

A neural network architecture designed for sequential data by maintaining information from previous inputs.

---

## Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

A technique that combines information retrieval with language generation so a model can use external knowledge sources when producing responses.

---

# S

## Sarcasm

A form of language where the intended meaning differs from the literal meaning, making it difficult for NLP systems to interpret accurately.

---

## Self-Attention

An attention mechanism in which each token attends to other tokens within the same input sequence.

---

## Semantic Meaning

The underlying meaning conveyed by words or sentences rather than just their literal form.

---

## Sentiment Analysis

An NLP task that identifies the emotional tone or opinion expressed in text.

---

## Sparse Vector

A numerical representation where most values are zero.

Bag of Words and TF-IDF produce sparse vectors.

---

## Speech Recognition

The process of converting spoken language into text.

---

## Stemming

The process of reducing words to their root or stem form, sometimes producing non-dictionary words.

---

## Stop Words

Common words such as **the**, **is**, and **and** that may be removed in some NLP pipelines, depending on the application.

---

## Subword Tokenization

A tokenization technique that splits words into smaller meaningful pieces, helping models handle rare or unknown words.

---

# T

## Term Frequency (TF)

The number of times a word appears in a document.

---

## TF-IDF

**Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency**

A numerical statistic that measures how important a word is within a document relative to a collection of documents.

---

## Token

The smallest unit of text processed by an NLP model.

Examples:

- Word
- Character
- Subword
- Sentence

---

## Tokenization

The process of splitting text into tokens.

---

## Transformer

A neural network architecture based on self-attention that processes text efficiently and forms the foundation of modern NLP models.

---

# U

## Unsupervised Learning

A Machine Learning approach in which models learn patterns from unlabeled data.

Many language models begin by learning from large collections of unlabeled text.

---

# V

## Value (V)

In the Attention Mechanism, the information associated with each token that contributes to the final output after attention scores are computed.

---

## Vector

A numerical representation of data.

Words, sentences, and documents can all be represented as vectors for Machine Learning models.

---

## Vocabulary

The collection of unique tokens known to a model or present in a dataset.

---

# W

## Word Embedding

A dense vector representation of words that captures semantic relationships between them.

---

## Word2Vec

A popular word embedding algorithm that learns vector representations of words based on their surrounding context.

---

## Workflow

A sequence of processing steps used to solve an NLP task.

Example:

```text
Raw Text

↓

Preprocessing

↓

Tokenization

↓

Embeddings

↓

Transformer

↓

Prediction
```

---

# X

## Explainable AI (XAI)

Methods and techniques that help humans understand how AI models make decisions.
