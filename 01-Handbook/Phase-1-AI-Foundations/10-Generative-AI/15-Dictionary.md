# 📚 Introduction

This dictionary provides an **A-to-Z glossary** of the most important terms used in Generative AI.

It is designed for:

- 📖 Learning new concepts
- 💼 Interview preparation
- 📝 Exam revision
- 🚀 Project development
- 💻 Daily reference

Each definition is written in **simple, beginner-friendly language**.

---

# 🔤 A

---

## AI Assistant

A software application powered by Artificial Intelligence that helps users perform tasks such as answering questions, generating content, writing code, or analyzing documents.

**Example**

- ChatGPT
- Microsoft Copilot
- Google Gemini

---

## AI Hallucination

A situation where an AI model generates information that sounds believable but is incorrect, unsupported, or fabricated.

**Example**

An AI invents a fake research paper or cites a source that does not exist.

---

## Algorithm

A step-by-step procedure used by computers to solve problems or perform tasks.

**Example**

Sorting numbers using a sorting algorithm.

---

## Attention

A mechanism used in Transformer models that helps the model determine which parts of the input are most important when generating an output.

---

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The field of computer science focused on building systems that perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, perception, and decision-making.

---

# 🔤 B

---

## Bias

A systematic tendency that causes AI outputs to unfairly favor or disadvantage certain groups, ideas, or outcomes because of patterns in the training data or system design.

---

## Batch

A group of training examples processed together during one training step.

---

## Benchmark

A standardized test or dataset used to evaluate the performance of AI models.

---

## Backpropagation

A training algorithm used in neural networks to update model parameters by propagating errors backward through the network.

---

# 🔤 C

---

## Chatbot

An AI application designed to communicate with users through natural language.

---

## Context

Background information provided to an AI model that helps it better understand a request.

---

## Computer Vision

A branch of AI that enables computers to interpret and analyze images and videos.

---

## Copyright

Legal protection granted to creators of original works. AI-generated content may involve copyright considerations depending on how it is created and used.

---

# 🔤 D

---

## Dataset

A collection of data used to train, validate, or evaluate AI models.

---

## Deep Learning

A branch of Machine Learning that uses deep neural networks to learn complex patterns from large datasets.

---

## Diffusion Model

A Generative AI model that creates images by learning to remove noise from random data through an iterative denoising process.

---

## Decoder

A neural network component that generates outputs from learned internal representations.

---

# 🔤 E

---

## Embedding

A numerical vector representation of data (such as text or images) that captures semantic meaning.

---

## Encoder

A neural network component that converts input data into internal numerical representations.

---

## Epoch

One complete pass through the entire training dataset during model training.

---

## Ethics

The principles that guide the responsible development and use of Artificial Intelligence.

---

# 🔤 F

---

## Fine-Tuning

The process of adapting a pre-trained model to perform better on a specific task using additional task-specific data.

---

## Foundation Model

A large pre-trained AI model that can be adapted to many downstream tasks.

---

## Few-Shot Prompting

A prompting technique where a small number of examples are included in the prompt to guide the model.

---

## Feedback Loop

A process where outputs are evaluated and used to improve future model behavior or system performance.

---

# 🔤 G

---

## GAN (Generative Adversarial Network)

A deep learning architecture consisting of a Generator and a Discriminator that compete during training to generate realistic synthetic data.

---

## Generative AI

A branch of AI that creates new content such as text, images, audio, video, software code, and documents.

---

## GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)

Specialized hardware commonly used to accelerate AI model training and inference through parallel computation.

---

# 🔤 H

---

## Hallucination

See **AI Hallucination**.

---

## Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

A workflow where humans review, guide, or validate AI-generated outputs before important decisions are made.

---

## Hyperparameter

A configuration value chosen before training begins, such as learning rate or batch size.

---

# 🔤 I

---

## Inference

The process of using a trained AI model to generate predictions or outputs for new inputs.

---

## Input

The information provided to an AI model.

Examples:

- Text
- Image
- Audio
- Video

---

## Image Generation

The process of creating new images using Generative AI models.

---

# 🔤 J

---

## JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)

A lightweight data format commonly used to exchange structured information between software applications and AI systems.

---

# 🔤 K

---

## Knowledge Base

A structured collection of information that can be searched or referenced by AI systems.

---

## Keyword

An important word or phrase used to describe a topic or improve information retrieval.

---

# 🔤 L

---

## Large Language Model (LLM)

A Foundation Model trained primarily on text that generates language by predicting the next token.

---

## Learning Rate

A training parameter that controls how much model parameters change after each update.

---

## Loss Function

A mathematical function that measures how far a model's predictions are from the expected results.

---

# 🔤 M

---

## Machine Learning

A branch of AI where computers learn patterns from data instead of being explicitly programmed for every task.

---

## Model

A trained AI system that performs specific tasks such as classification, prediction, or content generation.

---

## Multimodal AI

AI systems capable of processing multiple types of data such as text, images, audio, and video.

---

# 🔤 N

---

## Natural Language Processing (NLP)

A branch of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language.

---

## Neural Network

A computing model inspired by the structure of the human brain, consisting of interconnected artificial neurons.

---

## Next-Token Prediction

The process used by LLMs to generate text by predicting the most likely next token based on previous context.

---

# 🔤 O

---

## Overfitting

A situation where a model performs very well on training data but poorly on new, unseen data because it has learned training-specific details rather than general patterns.

---

## Optimization

The process of improving model parameters during training to reduce prediction errors.

---

# 🔤 P

---

## Parameter

A learned value within a model that is adjusted during training.

---

## Pattern Learning

The process by which AI models identify relationships and structures in training data.

---

## Prompt

The instruction, question, or input given to a Generative AI model.

---

## Prompt Engineering

The practice of designing and refining prompts to improve AI-generated outputs.

---

## Pretraining

The initial large-scale training phase in which a model learns broad knowledge from extensive datasets before being adapted to specific tasks.

---

# 🔤 Q

---

## Query

A request made to an AI system.

Example:

```text
Summarize this article.
```

---

## Quantization

A technique that reduces the size and computational requirements of AI models by representing parameters with lower numerical precision.

---

# 🔤 R

---

## Responsible AI

The practice of developing and using AI systems in ways that are ethical, safe, fair, transparent, and trustworthy.

---

## Reinforcement Learning

A Machine Learning approach where an agent learns by interacting with an environment and receiving rewards or penalties.

---

## Retrieval

The process of finding relevant information from a data source before or during AI-assisted tasks.

---

# 🔤 S

---

## Supervised Learning

A Machine Learning technique where models learn using labeled training data.

---

## Synthetic Data

Artificially generated data that resembles real-world data and can be used for training or testing AI systems.

---

## Sampling

The process of generating outputs from a trained generative model.

---

## Safety Filter

A mechanism designed to reduce unsafe, harmful, or inappropriate AI outputs.

---

# 🔤 T

---

## Token

A small unit of text processed by a language model. A token may represent a word, part of a word, punctuation, or another text element.

---

## Tokenization

The process of splitting text into tokens before it is processed by an AI model.

---

## Training

The process where an AI model learns patterns from data by adjusting its parameters.

---

## Transformer

A neural network architecture that uses attention mechanisms to efficiently process sequences and forms the basis of many modern LLMs.

---

# 🔤 U

---

## Unstructured Data

Data without a predefined format, such as free-form text, images, videos, and audio recordings.

---

## Unsupervised Learning

A Machine Learning technique where models discover patterns in unlabeled data.

---

# 🔤 V

---

## Validation Dataset

A dataset used during training to evaluate model performance and help tune hyperparameters without using the test dataset.

---

## Vector

An ordered list of numerical values used to represent information mathematically.

---

## Vector Database

A specialized database designed to store and search vector embeddings efficiently.

---

# 🔤 W

---

## Workflow

A sequence of steps used to complete a task.

Example:

```text
Prompt

↓

LLM

↓

Generated Response
```

---

## Weights

The learned numerical values within a neural network that determine how inputs are transformed into outputs.

---

# 🔤 X

---

## XAI (Explainable AI)

Techniques and methods that help people understand how AI systems make decisions or generate outputs.

---

# 🔤 Y

---

## Yield

In AI systems, yield generally refers to the useful output or results produced by a model or workflow. The exact meaning depends on the context.

---

# 🔤 Z

---

## Zero-Shot Prompting

A prompting technique where the model performs a task without being given examples in the prompt.

Example:

```text
Translate this sentence into French.
```

The model relies on knowledge acquired during training rather than example demonstrations within the prompt.
